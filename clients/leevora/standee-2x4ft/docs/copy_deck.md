# leevorá · Outdoor Standee Copy Deck

**Unit:** 2 ft x 4 ft double-sided LED backlit standee, on the pavement outside the clinic, Subhash Nagar.
**Reader:** walking or on a two-wheeler, 2 to 4 seconds, 8 to 10 m away.
**Rules held:** hooks are 6 words or fewer. No invented claims (no "dermatologist", prices, % results or rankings). None of the banned words. No em-dash pileups, no stacks of three adjectives.

---

## 1. Side A: Woman, Skin

| # | Hook | Words | Angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Your mirror only shows half. | 5 | Curiosity |
| 2 | What your mirror can't see. | 5 | Curiosity |
| 3 | Same face. Different story. | 4 | Curiosity |
| 4 | Five serums later. Still guessing? | 5 | Provocation |
| 5 | Reels don't know your skin. | 5 | Provocation |
| 6 | Filters lie. Light doesn't. | 4 | Provocation |
| 7 | See it first. Then treat it. | 6 | Positioning |
| 8 | We look before we treat. | 5 | Positioning |
| 9 | Don't treat what you haven't seen. | 6 | Positioning |
| 10 | Know exactly what your skin needs. | 6 | Benefit |
| 11 | Fewer products. The right ones. | 5 | Benefit |
| 12 | Your skin, in numbers. | 4 | Benefit / curiosity |

### Top 3

1. **Your mirror only shows half.** The picture finishes the sentence. The top half is what her mirror shows and the bottom half is the scan, so she gets the line and the image as one idea in one look.
2. **Five serums later. Still guessing?** It describes the shelf of half-used bottles in nearly every bathroom on this street, so she's nodding before she finishes reading.
3. **See it first. Then treat it.** Six one-syllable words that carry the whole positioning. Of the twelve, it's the easiest to read from a moving scooter.

**Pick for print: #1.** It's the only line that needs the picture, and the picture is the most expensive thing on the board, so the two should work together.

## 2. Side B: Man, Hair

| # | Hook | Words | Angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is your hairline moving back? | 5 | Curiosity |
| 2 | Seen your crown lately? | 4 | Curiosity |
| 3 | More hair in the drain? | 5 | Curiosity / recognition |
| 4 | Tried every oil? Try a scan. | 6 | Provocation |
| 5 | New shampoo. Same hair fall? | 5 | Provocation |
| 6 | Before your cousins notice. | 4 | Provocation |
| 7 | Maatha bada ho raha hai? | 5 | Provocation (Hinglish: "Is your forehead getting bigger?") |
| 8 | Don't wait for the bald patch. | 6 | Provocation |
| 9 | Hair fall has a reason. | 5 | Positioning |
| 10 | Check the roots, not the comb. | 6 | Positioning |
| 11 | Find out why it's falling. | 5 | Benefit |
| 12 | Hard water. Long days. Hair fall. | 6 | Local recognition |

### Top 3

1. **Hair fall has a reason.** It opens with "hair fall", the words he'd actually type into Google, and it promises an answer instead of another bottle. Five short words.
2. **Before your cousins notice.** What he dreads isn't the hair so much as the comment at the next family function. It's four words, and the scanned hairline beside it tells him what "notice" refers to.
3. **Tried every oil? Try a scan.** It names the onion-oil and bhringraj routine he's already on and makes the scan the obvious next step. Saying "Try" twice is what makes it stick.

**Pick for print: #9, "Hair fall has a reason."** On a premium board it's the safest of the three and still a strong one. Men walking past rarely respond to a riddle. They respond to their own problem said back to them, plainly.

**About the Hinglish:** "Maatha bada ho raha hai?" is the funniest line here and would get a laugh in Subhash Nagar. On a luxury lightbox, though, a Roman-script Hinglish joke drags the brand toward street poster. Use it on Instagram, WhatsApp Status or a reception counter card instead.

**About #2:** "Seen your crown lately?" only works if the design shows the crown. The face is front-on, so the crown isn't in shot. Keep it for a version that includes a crown inset.

## 3. Offer Line (big, gold)

The structure is two tiers: a big gold line with a smaller gold small-caps line under it. "Free" stays the first word, so it's the first thing anyone reads.

| # | Big line | Scarcity line |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Free Skin & Hair Analysis | LAUNCH MONTH ONLY |
| 2 | Free Skin & Hair Analysis | ONLY WHILE WE'RE NEW |
| 3 | Your first analysis is free | LAUNCH MONTH ONLY |
| 4 | Free, for our launch month | SKIN & HAIR ANALYSIS |
| 5 | FREE (set very large, on its own line) | Skin & Hair Analysis · Launch month only |

**Pick: #1.** It's plain and specific, and it reads like a clinic, not a sale. #2 sounds warmer but gives no time limit, so it does less work as scarcity.

**Match the order to the side.** Side A prints "Free Skin & Hair Analysis". Side B prints "Free **Hair & Skin** Analysis". It's the same offer, but the first noun matches the face. Otherwise a man reads "Skin" first and assumes the clinic is for women.

## 4. Proof Strip (3 items: up to 3 words each, plus a sublabel of up to 3 words)

**Option A: plain-spoken**
| Item | Sublabel |
|---|---|
| 8-Light Scan | What eyes miss |
| 17 Concerns | Pores to pigmentation |
| Expert-Led Plan | From your scan |

**Option B: numbers first**
| Item | Sublabel |
|---|---|
| 8 Lights | One clear picture |
| 17 Concerns | Mapped, not guessed |
| 1 Plan | Expert-led |

**Option C: verbs (recommended)**
| Item | Sublabel |
|---|---|
| See it | 8-light AI imaging |
| Measure it | 17 skin concerns |
| Treat it | Expert-led plan |

**Pick: C.** It puts the clinic's whole argument into six words, and it works on Side B by swapping the sublabels (see the final deck). The strip gets read by people who have already stopped, within about 3 m, so being clear matters more than being punchy. "AI" goes here and not in the hook: it's a real feature and it earns trust as a supporting detail, but as a headline it sounds like everyone else.

## 5. CTA (next to the QR code and phone number)

1. **Scan to book on WhatsApp**
2. Scan this. We'll scan you.
3. Point your camera here to book
4. Book your free analysis on WhatsApp
5. Say hi on WhatsApp. We'll fix a time.

**Pick: #1.** It's the clearest, and an older relative standing next to her will understand it too. Keep #2 for social posts, where people have time to enjoy the wordplay.

**Optional, only if the clinic takes walk-ins:** add "Or just walk in." under the CTA. The best CTA a pavement sign has is the clinic door two steps away. Don't print it unless walk-ins are actually handled.

## 6. Scan-Overlay Micro-Labels

**System rules:**
- Format: `LABEL · reading`, with the label in caps and the numerals in a tabular font.
- Each label gets a small ring marker and a hairline leader to the spot it describes.
- Skin readings are 0 to 100 scores, where a higher number means more of that concern.
- Keep the scores mid-range (12 to 52). The face should read as normal skin with things to work on, not as a problem face. Most passers-by will see themselves in that, and nobody feels shamed by the board.

## Production Notes

1. **Hook sizing.** Caps 6 to 8 cm tall can be read from 7 to 10 m. On a 2 ft panel that fits roughly 9 to 12 characters per line, so the hooks are set on two or three lines as shown above. Don't shrink them to fit one line.
2. **The 3-second read is only the hook and the offer**, about 10 words. Everything else is for people who have already stopped. Keep the hook and offer clearly bigger than everything else.
3. **Put "LAUNCH MONTH ONLY" on a separate vinyl strip.** The standee will stay up after the launch month, and a line that has expired is worse than no line. Peel it off and replace it, for example with "BOOK YOUR ANALYSIS".
4. **Side B scan direction.** The hairline sits above the eyes, so on Side B the line should cross the brow with the scan half on top. If the scan half is at the bottom, as on Side A, the hook and labels have nothing to point at.
5. **Check the Side B proof strip with the clinic.** "Scalp imaging" and "Density & thickness" assume the hair analysis includes close-up scalp imaging. If it doesn't, use: *Check it / Hair & scalp analysis · Understand it / Why it's falling · Treat it / Expert-led plan.*
6. **Micro-labels** should use the same UI typeface, with caps about 1.5 to 2 cm tall, in cyan at 70 to 80% opacity. They're texture for anyone within 2 m, and at night they're what makes the scan half look like a real machine.
7. **Deliberately left out:** "dermatologist", "doctor", prices, % outcomes, "No.1", "AI" in the headline, and "complimentary" (the street needs "Free").
